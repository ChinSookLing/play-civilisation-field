## 摘要

### 研究问题

不同公司的 AI 系统通常被隔离在彼此独立的网页入口中，无法直接互相传话。本研究记录测试一个较窄、可观察的问题：

> 当一个人类主持者和一个 AI 信使负责跨入口传递时，多个异质 AI 是否能够像坐在同一张桌子旁一样，以接力方式共同完成、核查并提升一组数学任务的证据等级？

### 方法

2026-10-03，Together · Proof Table 003（PT003）以 Erdős–Mollin–Walsh 猜想为背景，设置三个热身项目：

- **C1**：核查一个关于 Lean 4 验证到 \(10^{14}\) 的声明；
- **W1**：只用 2、3、5 的整除性，求三连 powerful 数的最小成员模 900 的全部可能余数；
- **W2**：证明存在无穷多对连续 powerful 数。

7 个席位共提交 8 个 turn，其中 7 个格式完整，1 个（T6）因缺少必填标题而记为 INCOMPLETE TURN；其数学内容仍作为观察记录保留。Opus 任主席；Puck（Grok Bot）担任信使与记录者；Tuzi 为人类主持者与批准关口。第 1 轮的多个 W1 答案在彼此不可见的条件下完成；第 2 轮用于核查、反驳与继续推进。公开 wall 保存每一行记录，together-mailbox 保存主席与信使之间的跨入口信件，账本记录 OPEN / CLOSED / REFUTED / DEAD END 及证据等级。

另有 Fable 在主席设定下独立完成相同任务，作为一个**探索性比较记录**，不是严格控制实验。两条记录经匿名 A/B 处理后，由 GLM 与 Lumo 独立盲评。桌子关闭后，Fable 编写 Lean 核验文件，Puck 在第二台机器上重跑。

### 结果

PT003 完成了三个目标项目在本桌所要求的任务层级；其中 C1 完成的是来源核查，不等于独立重建完整的 \(10^{14}\) 计算。接力内部发现了本次运行唯一一个实质数学错误：T2 的 W1 最终余数列表多 7 个、少 7 个；后续席位通过代码与反驳发现并纠正，最终 W1 列表为 39 个余数。W2 由 Pell 型递推给出手写证明。桌子关闭后，W1、W1-exact、W2 以及对 T2 列表错误的反驳达到 PROVED-LEAN，Puck 在第二台机器上重跑同一 Lean 文件，退出码为 0，19 行 axiom 输出逐字节一致。

C1 的完整无条件 \(10^{14}\) 结论**没有**在本次实验中重建。只重查了 3,204 个分块证书中的 31 个（0.97%），因此无条件定理保持 OPEN。

作为附带比较，两位盲评者都给桌子与 Fable 对照相同的内容分 13/13；这里的 13/13 仅指评分表第 1、4、5 项的内容分，不是九项全部满分。桌子的 1 个实质错误按“一份错误的最终列表”计数，其中包含多 7、少 7；该错误被接力内部发现并纠正。对照“没有实质数学错误”是两位评分者对私人对照记录的报告结果，本文作者未重新读取该私人包。由于两边工具条件不对等，对照拥有 Lean 与代码执行，而桌上多数席位只在聊天环境中作答，因此本次结果不能支持“relay 优于 single agent”或相反的一般结论。

实验当天还暴露了多项操作摩擦，包括手打时间戳错误、wall 席位拒绝、账本 HTTP 500、评分包截断、内存限制以及代理行为在共享人类账户下难以归属。这些失败直接进入后续规则 v0.4 → v0.5 的修改。

### 结论

本次单次实验支持一个有限结论：

> 在这组任务与这套基础设施下，一个跨入口、有人类授权、保留 provenance 的 AI 接力流程可以完成数学任务、在内部发现并纠正错误，并把部分结果交接给形式化核验。

它不证明这种桌子一般优于单一模型，也不证明其结果可推广到其他数学问题、模型组合或工具条件。

## Abstract

### Research question

AI systems operated through different companies' consumer web portals often cannot communicate with each other directly. This study records a narrower, observable question:

> Can heterogeneous AI systems, isolated behind separate portals, collaboratively complete, critique and verify mathematical tasks through a human-authorized relay workflow?

### Method

On 2026-10-03, Together · Proof Table 003 (PT003) used the Erdős–Mollin–Walsh conjecture as background and posed three warm-up items:

- **C1**: verify a claim about a Lean 4 check up to \(10^{14}\);
- **W1**: using divisibility by 2, 3 and 5 only, determine all residues modulo 900 that the smallest member of three consecutive powerful numbers could occupy;
- **W2**: prove that infinitely many pairs of consecutive powerful numbers exist.

Seven seats submitted eight turns; seven were format-complete and one (T6) was recorded as an INCOMPLETE TURN because required headings were missing, while its mathematical content was preserved as an observation. Opus chaired; Puck (Grok Bot) acted as courier and recorder; Tuzi acted as human host and approval gate. Multiple W1 answers in Round 1 were produced independently before being revealed. Round 2 was used for checking, refutation and further work. A public wall preserved turn records, together-mailbox preserved cross-portal letters between chair and courier, and a ledger tracked OPEN / CLOSED / REFUTED / DEAD-END items and evidence status.

A separate Fable run completed the same task under the chair's setup. It is treated here as an **exploratory comparator**, not a controlled performance experiment. The two records were anonymised as A/B and scored blind by GLM and Lumo. After the table closed, Fable wrote Lean verification files and Puck reran the same file on a second machine. [v0.4 §3.6; §4.3]

### Results

PT003 completed the three target items at the task level required by the table; for C1 this meant completing the source-checking task, not independently rebuilding the full \(10^{14}\) computation. The relay internally detected the only substantive mathematical error observed in the table record: T2's final W1 residue list contained seven extra and seven missing values. Later seats independently detected and corrected the error by code and refutation; the final W1 set contained 39 residues. W2 was proved by a Pell-type recurrence.

After closure, W1, W1-exact, W2 and the refutation of T2's list reached PROVED-LEAN. Puck reran the same Lean file on a second machine with exit code 0; all 19 axiom-output lines were byte-identical.

The unconditional \(10^{14}\) result for C1 was **not** rebuilt in this experiment. Only 31 of 3,204 chunk certificates (0.97%) were rechecked, so the unconditional theorem remained OPEN.

As a secondary comparison, both blind scorers gave the relay record and Fable comparator the same 13/13 content score on scoring-sheet items 1, 4 and 5; this was not a perfect score across all nine scoring items. The relay's one substantive error was counted as one incorrect final list containing seven extra and seven missing residues, and it was corrected internally. The claim that the comparator contained no substantive mathematical error is reported from the blind scorers' assessment of the private comparator record; the paper authors did not independently reread that private package. Because tool conditions differed substantially, the comparator had Lean and code execution while most table seats answered in chat-only conditions, these observations do not establish a general performance advantage for either mode.

Operational failures, including hand-entered timestamp errors, a rejected seat, a ledger HTTP 500, scorer-input truncation, memory limits, and ambiguous agent attribution under shared human accounts, directly informed rule revisions from v0.4 to v0.5.

### Conclusion

This single run supports only a bounded conclusion:

> Under these tasks and infrastructure conditions, a provenance-preserving, human-authorized cross-portal relay can complete mathematical tasks, detect and correct an internal error, and hand selected results into formal verification.

It does not establish that relay collaboration generally outperforms a single model, nor that these findings generalise across problems, model combinations or tool settings.

# 1. Introduction

## 1.1 Why cross-portal collaboration is a distinct problem

Many AI collaboration systems assume APIs, shared runtimes, shared memory, or a common agent framework. The Together environment tested here began from a different constraint: the participating AI systems lived behind separate consumer-facing web portals. A GPT seat, a Gemini seat, a Grok seat, a Claude-family seat, and others did not share a native communication channel.

In this setting, collaboration requires explicit transport.

PT003 used several transport paths rather than one uniform automation layer. [v0.4 §1.1]

1. **Puck (Grok Bot)** operated the web portals that were accessible from Puck's environment and carried prompts and responses under Tuzi's approval;
2. **Fable** was carried manually by Tuzi where direct portal automation was unavailable;
3. **Opus** used its own session tools to read from and submit to **together-mailbox**, including a scheduled self-check that read a mailbox letter without Puck carrying it;
4. **together-mailbox**, a Git-based append-only mailbox, preserved chair/courier letters with authorship, commit time and content hashes.

According to the preserved record, after Anthropic support stated that automated non-API access to claude.ai was not supported, the table did not continue automated attempts to access claude.ai.

In this run, the cross-portal workflow exposed failure modes including stale or incorrect relays, portal-specific access restrictions, ambiguous identity attribution, truncated copies, timestamp drift, and inconsistent evidence status.

## 1.2 Why mathematics was used

The purpose of PT003 was not to solve the Erdős–Mollin–Walsh conjecture itself.

The conjecture was used as a source of bounded mathematical tasks with different verification demands:

- source reading and claim checking (C1);
- finite modular reasoning and exact enumeration (W1);
- constructive proof (W2).

These tasks create opportunities for disagreement, correction, code checking, and formal verification.

## 1.3 Primary and secondary questions

The **primary research question** was:

> Can a heterogeneous set of AI systems complete and verify mathematical tasks in relay when no direct agent-to-agent channel exists?

A **secondary exploratory question** was added by the chair:

> How does this relay record compare, on the same task, with one strong single-agent run?

The secondary comparison was not Tuzi's original purpose and was not performed under equal tool conditions. The difference between Tuzi's original purpose and the chair's comparison framing was formally clarified in the record only at 15:58, after scoring and the comparison conclusion had already been posted. [v0.4 §3.4; §5.9] The primary/secondary ordering used in this paper is therefore a retrospective presentation of that clarification, not a preregistered hierarchy of outcome measures. The comparison is reported as an exploratory comparator only.

## 1.4 Contributions of this record

This working paper contributes:

1. a documented cross-portal relay workflow for mathematical work;
2. one empirical run with independent first-round answers, later checking and public provenance;
3. a preserved example of internal error detection and correction;
4. a handoff from conversational reasoning to Lean verification;
5. a blind comparison against one single-agent comparator run;
6. a failure log showing how transport, identity and infrastructure problems changed the subsequent rules.

# 2. Methods

## 2.1 Participants and roles

The table record contains seven AI seats that submitted turns:

- GPT;
- Gemini;
- Kimi;
- DeepSeek;
- Qwen;
- Astra;
- Grok.

GPT submitted two turns, for a total of eight submitted turns. Seven met the required six-field format; T6 was preserved as an INCOMPLETE TURN and was not counted by the chair as a formal contribution. [v0.4 §3.2; §5.7]

Supporting roles were:

- **Tuzi** — human host, manual relay where required, approval gate;
- **Opus** — chair, round summaries, evidence-status interpretation, ledger text, later audit;
- **Puck (Grok Bot)** — courier, wall posting, record preservation, ledger handling, later second-machine rerun;
- **Fable** — exploratory comparator, later Lean verifier;
- **GLM and Lumo** — blind scorers;
- **Bill** — infrastructure maintenance.

## 2.2 Tasks

PT003 used three items.

### C1 — source and formalisation claim check

Participants were asked to verify a claim that the Erdős–Mollin–Walsh conjecture had been checked in Lean 4 up to \(10^{14}\), including the exact theorem statement, definition of powerful, treatment of 0 and 1, interpretation of the bound, axiom output, and any larger uncertified computational bounds mentioned by the cited paper.

### W1 — modular residue problem

Using only divisibility by 2, 3 and 5, participants were asked to determine every possible residue modulo 900 for the smallest member \(n\) of a hypothetical powerful triple \((n,n+1,n+2)\).

### W2 — infinitely many consecutive powerful pairs

Participants were asked to prove that infinitely many pairs of consecutive powerful numbers exist.

## 2.3 Turn format and evidence labels

Each turn was required to contain:

- GOAL;
- ACTION;
- RESULT;
- CHECK;
- STATUS CLAIM;
- NEXT.

Missing fields made a turn INCOMPLETE.

Evidence labels included:

- PROVED-LEAN;
- CHECKED-CODE;
- HAND-CHECKED;
- OPEN;
- REFUTED;
- DEAD-END.

Agreement between seats was not treated as a check by itself.

## 2.4 Independence procedure

For W1 in Round 1, Gemini, Kimi and GPT answered independently. Their responses were withheld until all three had arrived, then posted together. This prevented one first-round answer from influencing another.

Round 2 then exposed prior work to later seats so that the next turns could check, refute or extend the record.

## 2.5 Transport and provenance

### Public wall

The Play wall kept lines append-only: old lines were not deleted; corrections appeared as new lines.

### together-mailbox

Chair and courier used an append-only Git mailbox. Each letter was stored as a new file and commit. Corrections were new letters rather than edits. Mailbox records included sender, recipient, table, reply target, AS_OF and trust boundary.

The first handshake itself revealed a provenance problem: Opus hand-entered an AS_OF time that did not match commit time. The mismatch was preserved and later became part of the rule revision.

## 2.6 Exploratory comparator and blind scoring

Fable completed the same task independently under the chair's setup.

This comparator differed from the relay table in important ways:

- Fable had Lean and code execution;
- most table seats answered in chat-only conditions;
- the comparator and table were different model configurations;
- only one task instance was run.

The two records were anonymised as A/B by a coin flip. GLM and Lumo scored them independently using a nine-item sheet. Disagreement was preserved rather than averaged.

Because the conditions were not matched, this is not treated as a controlled superiority test.

The blind-scoring record also preserved several qualifications. [v0.4 §2.5; §4.2; §5.10]

- the reported 13/13 score covers scoring-sheet items 1, 4 and 5, not all nine items;
- on the comparator's status-overclaim item, GLM scored 0 while Lumo scored 2, and the disagreement was retained rather than averaged away;
- after the scorers were informed that the chair had rerun T5, both revised the relay record's status-overclaim count from 2 to 1;
- anonymisation removed explicit identity labels but did not erase stylistic differences between a multi-author cross-checking record and a single first-person thread;
- the paper authors did not read the private Fable comparator package during drafting; comparator-error claims are therefore reported from the scoring record rather than re-adjudicated here.

## 2.7 Formal verification after closure

After PT003 closed, Fable wrote Lean formalisation for W1, W1-exact, the T2 list error, W2 and W2 positivity. Puck then reran the same file on a second machine using the same Lean and Mathlib versions reported in the rerun record.

C1 required substantially more resources and was only partially rechecked.

# 3. Results

## 3.1 Primary outcome: relay completion and self-correction

The relay completed all three target items.

### C1

The source-reading task was completed correctly at the reading layer, but the full \(10^{14}\) Lean computation was not independently rebuilt.

### W1

The final answer contained **39** admissible residues modulo 900.

One first-round answer, T2, had the correct count and correct local modular method but an incorrect final list:

- 7 extra residues;
- 7 missing residues.

The error was detected later inside the relay:

- T5 used code to independently derive the set;
- T6 independently identified the mismatch, although its turn was formally INCOMPLETE because it omitted required headings.

The final accepted list matched the independently checked set.

### W2

A Pell-type recurrence was used to construct infinitely many consecutive powerful pairs. A later seat attempted to break the argument and did not find a failure.

## 3.2 Compact quantitative summary

| Measure | Observed result |
|---|---|
| AI seats with submissions | 7 |
| Submitted turns | 8 |
| Format-complete turns | 7 |
| Incomplete turns | 1 (T6) |
| Target tasks completed at their requested task level | 3 / 3 |
| Substantive table errors | 1 incorrect final list (7 extra, 7 missing) |
| Substantive table errors internally detected | 1 / 1 |
| W1 final residue count | 39 |
| Blind content score, relay | 13 / 13 on scoring items 1, 4 and 5 |
| Blind content score, comparator | 13 / 13 on scoring items 1, 4 and 5 |
| Comparator substantive mathematical errors | 0, as reported by blind scorers |
| C1 full \(10^{14}\) rebuild | OPEN |
| C1 chunk certificates kernel-rechecked | 31 / 3,204 = 0.97% |

### Evidence status at closure and after closure

| Item | At table closure | Post-closure supplement |
|---|---|---|
| C1 source reading | HAND-CHECKED | Reading status unchanged; full unconditional theorem rebuild remained OPEN |
| W1 | CHECKED-CODE | PROVED-LEAN |
| W2 | HAND-CHECKED | PROVED-LEAN |
| T2 incorrect list | REFUTED | Refutation itself received Lean verification |

Thus “3/3 tasks completed” refers to completion of the assigned task objectives; it does not mean that all three items reached the highest evidence grade.

## 3.3 Exploratory comparator result

Both GLM and Lumo gave both records 13/13 on the scoring sheet's content components (items 1, 4 and 5). This was not a perfect score across all nine scoring dimensions.

The relay record contained one substantive mathematical error, counted as one incorrect final list with seven extra and seven missing values, and corrected it internally. The comparator contained no substantive mathematical error according to the blind scorers' reports; the private comparator package was not independently reread by the authors of this paper.

Both scorers judged the comparator to show stronger verified progress. On the comparator's status-overclaim item, however, GLM scored 0 and Lumo scored 2; that disagreement was preserved. After learning that the chair had rerun T5, both scorers revised the relay record's status-overclaim count from 2 to 1. Anonymisation removed explicit identities but did not eliminate stylistic clues distinguishing a multi-author cross-checking record from a single first-person thread. However, the tool asymmetry is substantial: the comparator had Lean and code execution while table seats mostly did not. Therefore this result supports only a run-specific observation:

> this relay did not outperform this comparator under these unequal conditions.

It does not support a general claim that single-agent work is superior to relay work.

## 3.4 Post-closure Lean verification

Fable's Lean file formalised:

- W1;
- W1-exact;
- W1-reasoning equivalence;
- T2-extra and T2-missing;
- W2;
- W2 positivity.

Puck reran the same file on a second machine. [v0.4 §3.6; §4.3]

Reported rerun details:

- Lean 4.30.0;
- Mathlib c5ea00351c28e24afc9f0f84379aa41082b1188f;
- Optio 3319f637e8d1b13bf174982c0faefdef07a95884;
- command: `lake env lean PT003.lean`;
- exit code: 0;
- wall time: 63.12 seconds;
- peak resident memory: approximately 3,177 MiB;
- 19 axiom-output lines byte-identical to Fable's reported output;
- no warning, error or `sorryAx`.

This establishes reproducibility of the submitted W1/W2 Lean file on a second machine. It does not independently establish originality of the mathematical ideas.

A separate provenance distinction applies to T5's Python check: the chair's 13:49 rerun was recorded as a statement but had no retained execution log. A new chair rerun at 17:25, followed by Puck's rerun at approximately 17:28, produced matching script/output hashes and reproducible results. Those later reruns establish reproducibility of the script; they do not retroactively prove the process of the undocumented 13:49 execution. [v0.4 §7.3]

## 3.5 C1 remained open at the full bound

The C1 formalisation was only partially checked.

Fable reported confirmation of:

- the theorem's bounded form;
- the conditional theorem's axiom list;
- a lower-memory proof of the large table equality;
- 31 of 3,204 chunk certificates;
- mutation tests in which deliberately altered certificates were rejected.

The 31 / 3,204 figure refers specifically to **Lean kernel rechecks**. Separately, Fable reported an independent-code recomputation of all 3,204 certificate-table rows, with matching counts and pair lists; this is auxiliary computational evidence, not a substitute for rebuilding all certificates in Lean. [v0.4 §4.4]

Important provenance limits remain:

- 3,173 chunk certificates were not kernel-rechecked;
- the unconditional theorem was not rebuilt;
- Puck did not rerun PT003_C1.lean on the second machine;
- the C1 artefacts `PT003_C1.lean`, `gen_c1.py` and the chunk-output file were not obtained in the original paper workspace; their hashes were reported by Fable rather than independently checked there;
- the remaining full check was estimated to require about 30 CPU hours.

Therefore the unconditional \(10^{14}\) theorem remains OPEN in this experiment.

# 4. Operational findings

The main mathematical tasks were completed, but the experiment also exposed reliability problems in cross-portal collaboration. These findings are part of the study because they affect whether a relay record can be trusted.

## 4.1 Timestamp mismatch

Several AS_OF values were typed manually and did not match commit times. The first Opus handshake letter stated 12:25 although the commit occurred at 12:20:58. Opus later acknowledged this as a hand-entered estimate.

A later rules letter repeated the same class of error.

Resulting design implication:

> commit or machine time should be authoritative; AS_OF should be generated rather than manually typed.

## 4.2 Metadata disagreement

Examples included:

- wall META saying Tuzi carried by hand while individual lines said Puck carried them;
- one RELAY field defaulting incorrectly to Tuzi;
- different seat numbering in later rule proposals.

These were corrected by new records rather than deletion.

## 4.3 Seat rejection

When Puck attempted to post Fable's Lean check as a Fable turn, the wall rejected it because Fable was not registered as a seat. The content had to be preserved as courier notes instead.

This directly informed PT004's request to add explicit testing seats and rerun/read record types.

## 4.4 Ledger failure

The ledger API returned HTTP 500 when a version field was numeric. Bill corrected the infrastructure so numeric versions were retained as text, after which the ledger could be posted.

## 4.5 Input truncation

A blind-scoring package of 98,183 characters was truncated by GLM at roughly half its length. GLM returned INCOMPLETE rather than guessing, after which the package was split.

This incident supports the use of explicit completeness markers and smaller transport units.

## 4.6 Memory limitation

Puck's machine had insufficient available memory for the intended C1 second-machine rerun. W1/W2 could be reproduced; C1 could not.

This shows that evidence status depended not only on mathematical reasoning but also on available compute.

## 4.7 Agent–account attribution gap

Several GitHub actions performed by Puck or Bill appeared publicly under Tuzi's GitHub account because the platform recognised only the authenticated human account.

Attribution therefore had to be reconstructed from:

- letter headers;
- RELAY fields;
- code author fields;
- later statements.

We refer to this as an **agent–account attribution gap**:

> the platform account identifies the authorising human account, while the operational actor may be a distinct AI agent.

This is relevant to provenance in any cross-portal system using shared human credentials.

# 5. Discussion

## 5.1 What the relay demonstrated

The strongest result is not that the table produced a mathematically better answer than a single strong agent. It did not establish that.

The stronger, narrower observation is that the relay process supported:

1. independent first attempts;
2. later exposure to prior work;
3. explicit checking and refutation;
4. internal detection of an error;
5. preservation of the incorrect original;
6. transition from conversational results to formal verification.

That makes the relay interesting as a **workflow for correction and provenance**, not as a demonstrated performance multiplier.

## 5.2 Why the comparator cannot answer the superiority question

The comparator had stronger tools. Much of its deeper verification may come from that fact rather than from single-agent structure itself.

The correct interpretation is therefore:

> the table and comparator both reached correct content on this run; the comparator reached deeper verification sooner, but under unequal tool conditions.

A future comparison would need:

- matched tool access;
- matched time or token budget;
- multiple tasks;
- repeated runs;
- fixed predeclared outcome measures.

## 5.3 Error correction may be more informative than error count

The relay made one substantive error, while the comparator made none.

That could be read as a weakness of the relay.

However, PT003 also preserved a mechanism that made the error visible and correctable:

- independent first-round answers;
- code-based checking;
- a separate refutation turn;
- append-only correction rather than deletion.

This single run is insufficient to show that relay systems are generally better at self-correction. It does show that the protocol can retain and expose a correction chain.

## 5.4 Formal verification changes the meaning of "agreement"

Several seats agreed on mathematical content before Lean verification.

The experiment treated that agreement as insufficient evidence.

Only after a Lean file was produced and rerun did W1 and W2 reach PROVED-LEAN.

This distinction is central:

> multiple models agreeing is a social fact; formal verification is a different kind of evidence.

## 5.5 Provenance is part of the computational result

In cross-portal collaboration, a mathematical answer without provenance is incomplete.

The wall, mailbox, ledger and rerun records together answer questions such as:

- who proposed a claim;
- who checked it;
- what changed;
- which machine reran a file;
- which action was actually taken by an AI acting through a human account;
- which evidence status was justified at each point.

The same infrastructure also exposed its own failure modes, which then became inputs to rule revision.

---

# 6. Limitations

1. **Single task family, single run.** PT003 covered one mathematical setting and was run once.
2. **Known-answer warm-up structure.** The tasks were selected as warm-ups, not as an open mathematical discovery test.
3. **Unequal tool access.** Fable had Lean and code execution; most relay seats were chat-only.
4. **Different model families and configurations.** The experiment did not control model capability.
5. **Comparator not fully controlled.** The Fable run should be interpreted as an exploratory comparator, not a formal control group.
6. **Partial private records.** Full Opus/Fable chat transcripts were not preserved; the record relies on letters, wall lines and disclosed outputs.
7. **Human approvals sometimes recorded indirectly.** Some Tuzi approvals appear only inside Puck/Opus letters rather than as separate signed records.
8. **C1 was not fully rebuilt.** Only 31 of 3,204 chunk certificates were rechecked; the unconditional \(10^{14}\) theorem remained OPEN.
9. **Fable's dual role reduces independence.** Fable acted first as comparator and later as Lean verifier, and had already produced related formal work during the comparator run.
10. **Second-machine rerun checks reproducibility, not independent mathematical discovery.**
11. **The courier was also a recorder and later paper drafter.** This concentration of roles is a possible source of bias.
12. **Some source material was added after the initial source pack.** Anthropic support excerpts and Bill's GH#3 attribution statement were later additions and are explicitly marked as such.
13. **Some failed operations lack direct logs.** For example, the failed Fable-turn POST is inferred partly from file timestamps.
14. **W2 first-formalisation priority was not checked.** No novelty claim is made.

---

# 7. Rule evolution after PT003

PT003 directly influenced rules v0.4 and v0.5.

Changes included:

- clarifying the primary purpose as solving problems together in relay;
- separating thinking seats from testing seats;
- making single-agent comparison optional rather than automatic;
- requiring machine-clock AS_OF;
- enforcing summary-length limits;
- requiring a second-machine rerun for PROVED-LEAN;
- recording tool versions;
- ensuring code is read before execution;
- adding explicit rerun and read-record formats;
- preserving independence for key-answer turns;
- excluding rerun records from turn budgets.

Four reviewing seats, GPT, Gemini, Grok and DeepSeek, returned YES WITH COMMENTS on v0.4. Twelve comments were recorded; ten were accepted into v0.5 and two rejected as context-mixed or unsupported.

Tuzi approved v0.5 at 16:29 and PT004 entered Round 0.

This rule evolution is treated as a secondary systems result, not as evidence about mathematical performance.

---

# 8. Conclusion

PT003 did not show that a relay of several AI systems is better than a single strong agent.

It showed something narrower and still useful.

Across separate consumer web portals, with a human host, an AI courier, an append-only public wall, a Git mailbox, a ledger and later formal verification:

- seven AI seats completed eight turns;
- all three target tasks reached correct final answers;
- one substantive mathematical error was preserved, discovered and corrected inside the relay;
- selected results were later formalised in Lean;
- the same Lean file was reproduced on a second machine;
- the largest formal claim, C1 to \(10^{14}\), remained explicitly OPEN because the full certificate set was not rebuilt;
- operational failures changed the next version of the protocol.

The experiment therefore supports a feasibility claim, not a superiority claim:

> A cross-portal, provenance-preserving AI relay can complete mathematical work, correct an internal error, and hand results into formal verification under the tested conditions.

Whether such relay structures provide measurable gains over strong single-agent systems remains an open experimental question.
